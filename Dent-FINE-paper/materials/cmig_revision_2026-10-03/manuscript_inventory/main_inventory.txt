P000 [block 0 / Title] DENT-STRIDE: Domain-Configured Structured FDI Tooth Detection Across Panoramic Radiographs and Intraoral Photographs
P001 [block 1 / Normal] Jiehui Sun*, Xi Wu*, Ruixin Tang, Pei Wu, Shiquan Min, Jiangping Zhu#, Ruijie Huang#
P002 [block 2 / Caption] Author affiliations and correspondence are provided in the accompanying title page.
P003 [block 3 / Heading 1] Abstract
P004 [block 4 / Normal] Automatic tooth detection and Fédération Dentaire Internationale (FDI) numbering must accommodate heterogeneous acquisition domains without confusing tooth-level accuracy with chart-level consistency. We present DENT-STRIDE, a configurable DEIM–D-FINE framework that separates factorized identity supervision and prototype auxiliary learning from training-derived anatomical priors and cross-scale feature aggregation. Panoramic radiographs, smartphone photographs, and five-view digital single-lens reflex (DSLR) photographs were studied using FDI-labelled bounding-box average precision (AP). The selected Pano, Mobile, and DSLR recipes achieved validation AP values of 54.385, 55.701, and 73.115, compared with 52.746, 53.369, and 72.560 for local reference recipes. These are single-run, recipe-level comparisons; Mobile uses a larger input resolution. A fixed-checkpoint panoramic replay increased AP from 53.783 to 54.385 when the combined center and shape prior was enabled. A separate 16-configuration feature-module study did not support monotonic gains from adding modules. Direct photographic cross-device transfer remained weak: Mobile-to-DSLR and DSLR-to-Mobile AP were 28.533 and 8.885. On smartphone images, matched-tooth FDI accuracy was 92.03%, but 35.45% of images contained repeated raw predicted labels. Identified image-content overlap in the panoramic and smartphone development splits further limits generalization claims. DENT-STRIDE therefore provides a domain-configured framework and an explicit analysis of the distinction between identity learning, geometric correction, and numbering consistency, rather than evidence of a universally optimal architecture or clinically autonomous charting.
P005 [block 5 / Caption] Keywords: tooth detection; FDI numbering; structured prediction; panoramic radiography; intraoral photography; transformer detector; domain shift
P006 [block 6 / Heading 1] 1. Introduction
P007 [block 7 / Normal] Accurate tooth identification supports diagnosis, treatment planning, and structured dental records. The FDI two-digit notation encodes dentition, quadrant, and tooth position (International Organization for Standardization, 2016). Ambiguous numbering can contribute to tooth-position errors, particularly when teeth are missing (Cullingham et al., 2017; Jacob et al., 2021; Pemberton, 2019). Automated localization and labelling offer reproducible charting inputs, although detector accuracy alone does not establish clinical benefit.
P008 [block 8 / Normal] Panoramic radiographs provide a comparatively standardized global view, whereas intraoral photographs vary in framing, viewpoint, occlusion, illumination, and reflection. Smartphone acquisition supports remote review but introduces substantial image variability (Alavi et al., 2025; Moharrami et al., 2024). Standardized five-view DSLR photography reduces some variability without eliminating missing teeth, mixed dentition, or similar neighbouring teeth. Fig. 1 illustrates these acquisition conditions and representative numbering errors.

P011 [block 11 / Normal] Localization, identity, and chart consistency are distinct endpoints. FDI-aware average precision requires both a correct box and a correct label, but does not ensure globally unique numbering. Similarly, a geometric prior may improve class ranking without improving learned appearance representations. We therefore separate identity supervision, geometric correction, and output consistency.
P012 [block 12 / Normal] DENT-STRIDE configures a common DEIM–D-FINE detector for each acquisition cohort. Factorized heads and a prototype bank provide structured identity supervision; SpatialShape supplies class-specific center and box-shape priors; cross-scale mutual feature aggregation (CMFA) changes feature fusion; and DualOptAdamW changes training optimization. Configuration occurs during development rather than through automatic test-time adaptation.
P013 [block 13 / Normal] The contributions are threefold: a query-level identity mechanism that separates factor-score fusion from prototype-only supervision; a distinct training-derived anatomical scoring model; and a multi-cohort evaluation combining detector comparisons, module combinations, controlled prior replay, resolution sensitivity, cross-device transfer, and numbering-consistency analysis. Together, these experiments examine acquisition-specific benefits and the limits of combining additional components.
P014 [block 14 / Heading 1] 2. Related work
P015 [block 15 / Heading 2] 2.1. Tooth localization and FDI numbering
P016 [block 16 / Normal] Tooth detection and numbering have been studied in panoramic radiographs (Tuzoff et al., 2019; Muramatsu et al., 2021; Yüksel et al., 2021), bitewings (Yaren Tekin et al., 2022), digital impressions (van Nistelrooij et al., 2025), and CBCT (Zhou et al., 2025). DENTEX combines enumeration and abnormal-tooth detection (Hamamci et al., 2023), while Yoon et al. (2024) studied tooth-number and caries detection in photographs. These settings differ in field of view, dentition coverage, and annotation conventions.
P017 [block 17 / Heading 2] 2.2. Structured identity and anatomical priors
P018 [block 18 / Normal] Label-tree cascades decompose tooth identity (Zhang et al., 2018), and prior-knowledge optimization can refine panoramic tooth/prosthesis detection (Ali et al., 2023). DENT-STRIDE distinguishes supervised FDI factors and class-conditional query prototypes from fixed box-geometry templates within a set-prediction detector. The former shape learned identity representations; the latter adjust class scores. Neither mechanism alone imposes a globally consistent dental chart.
P019 [block 19 / Heading 2] 2.3. Query detectors and multiscale representation
P020 [block 20 / Normal] D-FINE refines box-regression distributions (Peng et al., 2025), and DEIM improves transformer matching and convergence (Huang et al., 2025). RT-DETR (Zhao et al., 2023), RT-DETRv2 (Lv et al., 2024), Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2015), Ultralytics YOLO (Jocher et al., 2023), CAF-YOLO (Chen and Lu, 2025), and Dome-DETR (Hu et al., 2025) provide complementary detector references. Their native training and output processing differ; cross-framework comparisons therefore evaluate complete systems rather than an isolated architectural replacement.
P021 [block 21 / Heading 1] 3. Methods
P022 [block 22 / Heading 2] 3.1. Common detector and domain configurations
P023 [block 23 / Normal] The common scaffold comprises an HGNetv2-B0 backbone, three 256-channel projected feature levels, a hybrid encoder, and a three-layer D-FINE decoder with 300 ordinary queries. DEIM supplies matching and detection losses (Huang et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2025). Fig. 2 shows the component family. Pano uses factor/prototype auxiliary supervision and SpatialShape; Mobile uses CMFA, HOOA, factor-score fusion, and prototype supervision; DSLR uses CMFA with DualOptAdamW. Optional comparison refiners are detailed in Supplementary Section S1.

P057 [block 57 / Heading 2] 3.4. Cross-scale feature aggregation and local refinement
P058 [block 58 / Normal] CMFA (AdaptiveInterleaveFusion) replaces four adjacent-scale fusion sites: two top-down FPN and two bottom-up PAN sites (Fig. 4). Aligned inputs A and B each have 256 channels. Their concatenation is globally pooled and passed through 1×1 Conv–SiLU–1×1 Conv–Sigmoid to obtain channel gates g_A and g_B. Cross-enhanced features A′ = A + g_B⊙B and B′ = B + g_A⊙A are concatenated. Channel-wise mean/max maps then drive a 7×7 convolution and sigmoid spatial gate G_s. The gated 512-channel output feeds the next fusion block.

    IMAGES: [{"relationship": "rId18", "target": "media/image8.png"}]
P060 [block 60 / Caption] Fig. 4. CMFA: cross-gated adjacent-scale exchange followed by spatial gating. The lower panels identify its four insertion sites and summarize the operations. CMFA is active in the selected Mobile and DSLR configurations, but not in Pano.
P061 [block 61 / Normal] Mobile also uses HOOA after encoder aggregation (Fig. 5). Local, dilated-context, high-pass, and multiplicative local–context responses are projected, channel-gated, and spatially gated before a residual update bounded by 0.30 × sigmoid(s). HOOA is absent from the selected DSLR model. CEDv2, SMWG, MPF, and UCM are comparison refiners rather than components required by every configuration (Supplementary Section S1).

P064 [block 64 / Heading 2] 3.5. Training objective and optimization
P065 [block 65 / Normal] Training combines the native detection losses with configuration-dependent auxiliary objectives. Besides factor and prototype supervision, the local implementation uses an arch-position term; Mobile also uses a normalized Wasserstein-distance box term. With inactive terms set to zero, the objective is
P066 [block 66 / Normal]     (11)
    EQUATIONS: 1 L = L_det + λ_arch L_arch + Σ_h λ_h L_factor,h + L_proto + λ_NWD L_NWD
P067 [block 67 / Normal] Here L_det = L_MAL + 5L_L1 + 2L_GIoU + 0.15L_FGL + 1.5L_DDF. The prototype loss in Eq. (5) already includes its coefficient and is not weighted a second time. Intermediate decoder and denoising supervision follow the corresponding detector implementation.
P068 [block 68 / Normal] The training-only arch-position term groups matched teeth by reference vertical center, maps supported FDI identities to arch order, and penalizes the absolute difference from min–max-normalized predicted horizontal position. A small stabilizer prevents division by zero. This is a horizontal-order heuristic, not a fitted arch curve or an inference uniqueness constraint; its weights are 0.25 for Pano/DSLR and 0.10 for Mobile.
P069 [block 69 / Normal] For Mobile, the box-distance term uses normalized center c and width–height vector s. For each matched prediction and target, d = √(‖c − c*‖² + ‖s − s*‖²/4), and L_NWD is the mean of 1 − exp(−d/0.08). Its weight is 0.50. The Pano and DSLR active loss lists do not include this term. Factor cross-entropy weights for quadrant/position/dentition are 0.10/0.10/0.05 in Pano and 0.08/0.08/0.03 in Mobile. These auxiliary terms are part of the reported recipes and cannot be omitted from a reproducibility description.
P070 [block 70 / Normal] DualOptAdamW performs an AdamW step followed by rank-scaled post-step decay and rollback toward initialization (Ning et al., 2026). The DSLR recipe uses rollback 0.005, real-time decay 0.01, layer-decay power 1.5, and scaling bounds 0.5–1.5. Other selected recipes use AdamW. These changes affect optimization, not inference architecture.

P077 [block 78 / Heading 2] 4.2. Implementation and recipe accounting
P078 [block 79 / Normal] Selected models use seed 0, 160 epochs, mixed precision, training/validation batches of 8/16, exponential moving averages, and maximum-validation-AP checkpoint selection. AdamW uses learning rates 5 × 10⁻⁴ (base) and 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ (backbone), betas (0.9, 0.999), and weight decay 10⁻⁴. FlatCosine combines warm-up, a flat phase, cosine decay, and terminal low-learning-rate/no-augmentation training. Geometric flips are disabled to preserve FDI semantics. P082 [block 84 / Heading 2] 4.3. Evaluation endpoints and comparison protocols
P083 [block 85 / Normal] The primary endpoint is FDI-labelled COCO bounding-box AP over IoU thresholds 0.50:0.05:0.95. A correct box with an incorrect FDI label is not a correct-class true positive. AP50, AP75, and AR100 are secondary endpoints, reported in percentage points. DEIM-family outputs retain native top-300 predictions without added NMS or FDI-uniqueness constraints. External detectors retain native training and post-processing. Parameter counts describe executable training models, including auxiliary branches where present.
P084 [block 86 / Normal] Mobile threshold analysis uses score ≥0.30 and class-agnostic Hungarian assignment maximizing total IoU. A pair is correct only if IoU ≥0.50 and FDI identity agrees. TP counts correct pairs; FP and FN equal prediction and reference counts minus TP. Precision, recall, and F1 are micro-averaged. Matched-tooth FDI accuracy is correct identities/localization matches; coverage is matches/reference teeth. Duplicate-image rate is the proportion with a repeated predicted FDI label. These raw-output diagnostics are not clinical charting-error rates.
P085 [block 87 / Normal] Selected-recipe results are single-run validation-selected estimates. Fixed-checkpoint replay changes only the specified inference switch. Historical ablation families are interpreted within their own recipes rather than pooled with final models (Supplementary Table S6). Three-seed upstream baseline results are reported separately in Supplementary Section S4. The fixed threshold is exploratory, not clinically calibrated; selected-model confidence intervals are not estimated.
P086 [block 88 / Normal] Figs. 6–7 plot the reported numerical records. Source data and Python/Matplotlib visualization methods, including plotting-code assistance, are described in Supplementary Section S7.

P127 [block 134 / Normal] The standardized RTX 4090 implementation benchmark compares the local reference and a DSLR CMFA architecture: 12.631/12.633 GMACs, 5.91/6.30 ms mean forward latency, 169.2/158.7 images/s, and 140.8/142.2 MiB inference memory. P128 [block 135 / Heading 1] 5. Discussion
P129 [block 136 / Heading 2] 5.1. Principal findings and methodological scope
P130 [block 137 / Normal] Three findings distinguish mechanism from configuration. The fixed-checkpoint panoramic replay identifies a combined geometric score benefit. The complete feature family shows that stacking refiners can reduce AP. The resolution comparison explains a substantial part of Mobile recipe variation. Together, these results favour acquisition-specific configuration over a universal all-module design.
P131 [block 138 / Normal] Earlier label-tree and prior-knowledge systems already encode identity and anatomy (Zhang et al., 2018; Ali et al., 2023). DENT-STRIDE brings factor-score fusion, prototype-only supervision, and fixed geometric priors into a common query detector while keeping their roles separate. Representation visualizations support this interpretation, but do not establish that every gate, four prototypes, or the selected temperature is necessary or optimal.
P132 [block 139 / Heading 2] 5.2. Acquisition dependence and clinical interpretation
P133 [block 140 / Normal] The training-center patterns in Fig. 3 provide an interpretable basis for panoramic priors; photographic framing is less standardized. Opposing prior effects in the progressive family are consistent with acquisition dependence, without isolating geometry as the cause. Weak direct photographic transfer further distinguishes a framework trained separately by cohort from domain generalization without target training.
P134 [block 141 / Normal] The conditions and errors in Fig. 1 also motivate separate localization, identity, and image-level endpoints. The Mobile analysis confirms that correct matched-tooth labels need not yield a unique visible-field chart. Clinical use would require prespecified handling of missing teeth, mixed dentition, overlapping views, duplicates, and uncertainty, followed by independent evaluation of the complete charting workflow.
P135 [block 142 / Heading 2] 5.3. Limitations
P136 [block 143 / Normal] Selected results use one seed and validation-based configuration/checkpoint selection, without independent testing. DENTEX/smartphone content overlap may inflate development estimates, and absent patient identifiers prevent verification of patient-level independence. These limitations restrict generalization claims even when detector metrics improve.
P137 [block 144 / Normal] Recipes differ in resolution, augmentation, optimization, and auxiliary losses. The historical feature and identity families are not factorial ablations of all three final models, and individual center/shape, gating, factor-fusion, and prototype design choices are not independently resolved. External models retain native pretraining/post-processing, while historical photographic annotations differ in serialized form; these comparisons do not isolate architecture alone.
P138 [block 145 / Normal] RT-DETR-L has higher DSLR AP, so the selected DSLR advantage is compactness rather than unrestricted accuracy leadership. Unsupported FDI categories constrain prototype interpretation. Finally, illustrative cases, feature responses, and raw-query diagnostics do not establish autonomous charting safety or clinical utility.
P139 [block 146 / Heading 1] 6. Conclusion
P140 [block 147 / Normal] DENT-STRIDE separates structured FDI identity learning, training-derived anatomical scoring, and cross-scale aggregation within a common query detector. Selected Pano, Mobile, and DSLR recipes achieve 54.385, 55.701, and 73.115 AP under the reported development/validation protocols. Controlled prior replay, complete feature combinations, and resolution comparisons expose distinct contributors to these results. Weak cross-device transfer and duplicate labels despite high matched-tooth accuracy limit broader interpretation. The framework supports acquisition-specific tooth detection; independent, matched-protocol and chart-level evaluation are required for stronger generalization or clinical claims.
