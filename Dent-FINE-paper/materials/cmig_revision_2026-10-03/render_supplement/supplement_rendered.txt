S1. Optional feature-processing components
The main article shows HOOA in the Mobile configuration (Fig. 5). Figs. S1–S3 describe CEDv2,
SMWG, MPF, and UCM, which were evaluated in earlier feature-processing families and are not active
in the three domain-configured models of the controlled study.
CEDv2 operates on projected features before the main encoder. Coarse features are upsampled and
laterally projected to provide sigmoid guidance for the adjacent finer level; aligned branches are
concatenated and refined. The bottom-up path fuses learned stride-2 features reciprocally with coarser
branches (Fig. S1).




Fig. S1. CEDv2 comparison architecture. Guided top-down and bottom-up operations refine the projected pyramid before the
main encoder. CEDv2 is inactive in the domain-configured models. ↑ denotes resizing/upsampling except the labelled learned
stride-2 operation; C denotes concatenation, σ sigmoid, and ⊙ element-wise multiplication.

The auxiliary SMWG refiner acts on projected P3/P4/P5 before encoder aggregation. A pre-projection is
transformed with rFFT2 into a complex spectrum. Fixed radial low-, mid-, and high-frequency masks are
mixed by per-channel softmax weights and applied through the bounded modulation 1 + 0.20 × tanh(M).
After irFFT2 and post-projection, a dynamic channel gate computed from the pre-FFT feature controls a
residual update with α_s = 0.35 × sigmoid(s). Fine-to-coarse guidance computes a local high-pass
residual P−AvgPool3×3(P), resizes and projects it to the next coarser level, predicts a sigmoid guide, and
applies depthwise 3×3 refinement with a bounded residual update. The evaluated implementation is
Fourier-based and does not perform a discrete wavelet decomposition (Fig. S2).

Fig. S2. SMWG, an optional Fourier spectral-modulation and fine-to-coarse detail-guidance refiner. This evaluated

After FPN/PAN aggregation, the auxiliary MPF and UCM refiners can be applied independently to each
output level. MPF combines a depthwise 5×5 local-spatial branch, a global-average-pooled channel-
reweighting branch, and a 1×1→3×3 context branch using three softmax-normalized weights; the mixture
is injected with α_m=0.35×sigmoid(s). UCM computes μ=AvgPool₅×₅(x) and v=ReLU[AvgPool₅×₅(x²)
−μ²]. A learned uncertainty map U interpolates a depthwise detail update D from x−μ and a projected
context update C from μ, yielding R=U⊙ D+(1−U)⊙ C and P_ucm=x+α_uR with α_u=0.35×sigmoid(s).
Fig. S3 reflects this per-level refinement view, and when both are enabled MPF precedes UCM on each
level.

S7.1. Predictions and operating-point definitions
Validation image identifiers align predictions with the corresponding unchanged images and ground truth.
Clinical FDI codes are decoded through each cohort’s label map; zero-based category indices are not

                                                                       DENT-STRIDE | Supplementary material



displayed as tooth numbers. COCO AP uses the native detector outputs with no additional display score
cutoff. Fixed-threshold endpoints use score ≥0.30 and class-agnostic one-to-one Hungarian assignment
that maximizes total IoU, accepting only pairs with IoU ≥0.50. Exact FDI agreement defines a correct
detection. Wrong-number matches contribute both a false positive and a false negative. The denominators
for FDI accuracy, recall, localization coverage, and repeated-label image rate are explicitly distinguished
in Table S12.
Same-image qualitative panels use controlled seed-0 checkpoints, stored external predictions, the same
image, a common display threshold, consistent FDI decoding, and shared target crops. Examples include
favorable and adverse changes. Pano prior panels recompute scores from all ordinary candidates before
native top-k selection, switching only center and/or shape scoring for the same checkpoint. Selected
examples explain local mechanisms and do not estimate population frequencies of benefit or harm.

S7.2. Internal tensors and structured-identity diagnostics
Main Figs. 13–15 derive from the controlled Mobile combined seed-0 checkpoint. Four learned
prototypes per FDI class are L2-normalized; class centroids are formed by averaging then renormalizing.
Cosine similarity is computed between supported class centroids. Within-class cohesion is the mean
pairwise cosine similarity of the four prototypes. Unsupported FDI slots are masked, rather than
interpreted as learned representations.
Auxiliary-head confusion matrices use unique ordinary queries, retaining a query when its maximum
sigmoid class score is at least 0.30. Class-agnostic Hungarian matching against ground-truth boxes
accepts IoU ≥0.50 and yields 2,335 localized queries. This query-level subset differs from the post-top-k
detection denominator in Table S12, because native top-k outputs can retain multiple class scores from
one query. Factor labels follow quadrant 1–8, within-quadrant position 1–8, and dentition as primary or
permanent. Confusion values are normalized by the reference row; these branches are not objectness
heads.
CMFA and HOOA tensors are captured during an actual forward pass of the same seed-0 checkpoint.
CMFA channel gates gA and gB each contain 256 channel values displayed on a 16 × 16 grid; grid
location indexes channels and has no anatomical spatial meaning. The CMFA spatial gate is the actual
scalar gate at each feature location. HOOA panels show local, context, high-pass, and multiplicative
local–context responses, channel gates, spatial gates, input, and output. Response maps summarize root-
mean-square magnitude across channels and are normalized independently for display, while sigmoid
gate values retain their [0,1] scale. These maps are internal responses, not Grad-CAM, and do not
substitute for retrained causal ablations.

S7.3. Statistical and graphical conventions
Open symbols in repeated-seed plots represent actual seed outcomes; mean markers and error bars show
the mean and sample standard deviation. Seed-paired differences and df = 2 Student-t intervals are
calculated from the three actual within-seed differences. No error bars are added to single-run external or
internal results. Missing completed measurements are shown as “—” and are excluded from calculations.
No smoothing, interpolated training results, synthetic predictions, or generated clinical image content is
used.
Figures use a common Times New Roman typeface, restrained colors, and concise subplot labels;
descriptions and thresholds are placed in captions. Performance figures are generated from numerical
records and prediction arrays, and response/prototype figures from captured tensors. Standalone high-
resolution raster, vector PDF, and SVG sources accompany the figure assets. Clinical pixels, detection
boxes, identities, and gate values are not retouched.

S8. Controlled experiments: detailed results
The controlled study comprises 14 training configurations, each evaluated with seeds 0, 1, and 2. All use
the original cohort partitions, 160 recorded epochs, a training batch size of 8, a validation batch size of
16, mixed-precision training, and exponential moving average weights. The checkpoint with maximum
validation COCO AP is frozen and replayed in FP32. Within each cohort, data, initialization, common
losses, augmentation, optimization schedules, and checkpoint selection are held fixed except for the stated
variable. Mobile resolution is an explicit experimental factor. Pano training comparisons disable factor-
score fusion and all anatomical priors; the prior comparison is a subsequent same-weight replay. These
are internal-validation estimates.
